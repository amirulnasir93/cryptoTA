# Assessment method

How to turn gathered data into a horizon read without drifting into invention.

## The four reads

**Constructive** — several independent indicators appropriate to this horizon point
the same way. Not one strong signal; several that don't share a cause. Rising volume
and rising price are not independent.

**Neutral** — evidence is mixed, offsetting, or thin. This is the honest default and
should be the most common read. If most tokens on a list come back Constructive,
the method has drifted toward telling the user what they want.

**Cautious** — a specific identifiable headwind lands inside this window. Name it.
"Cautious" without a named cause is just hedging.

**Not assessable** — the data-quality or liquidity gate failed. State which.

## Deriving levels

Levels should have a reason attached, not be drawn from vibes. Legitimate sources:

- Prior swing highs and lows on the relevant timeframe
- Range boundaries where price has repeatedly reacted
- All-time high, and the drawdown from it
- TGE or launch price — psychologically live for recent tokens, and often where
  early buyers are underwater
- Round numbers, which matter more in crypto than they should
- Large visible liquidity or liquidation clusters where available
- Unlock price references — where a vesting tranche was priced

Say why a level matters. "Support at $0.42" is noise. "Support at $0.42, the range
low held three times since June" is usable.

## Catalyst handling

Build a dated catalyst list per token, then map each to the horizons it falls
inside. Categories worth checking:

- Token unlocks and vesting cliffs — the highest-signal scheduled event
- Exchange listings or delistings
- Product launches, mainnet upgrades, migrations
- Governance votes with a closing date
- Emission schedule changes
- Competitor launches in the same niche
- Macro events with crypto sensitivity

A material unlock inside the window generally dominates the technical picture at
that horizon. Supply arriving is mechanical; structure is inferred. Size the unlock
as a percentage of circulating supply — a 5% unlock and a 40% unlock are different
kinds of event, and the latter reprices the asset.

## Invalidation

Every non-neutral read needs a falsifying condition, stated as something
observable: a level breaking, volume failing to confirm, TVL continuing to decline
through a threshold, a catalyst passing without effect.

This matters more than the read itself. A read without invalidation cannot be
wrong, which means it cannot be useful either. If you cannot state what would
falsify it, the honest output is Neutral.

## Confidence and its limits

Express confidence in the *assessment*, never as a probability of profit. "High
confidence in the data, low confidence in direction" is a coherent and common
position. "70% chance this goes up" is fabricated precision and must not appear.

Confidence should track: how many independent sources agree, how complete the
picture is, how far the horizon extends beyond what the data supports, and whether
the token has enough history to establish structure at all. Tokens launched within
the past few months have no meaningful long-horizon technical structure — say that
rather than reading tea leaves on forty candles.

## Common failure modes

Watch for these in your own output:

- **Horizon smearing** — citing monthly fundamentals to support a 4h read, or
  dismissing a monthly thesis on intraday noise.
- **Correlated stacking** — issuing the same read across five names in one cluster
  and presenting it as five findings.
- **Recency capture** — treating the most recent move as the trend. A token up 20%
  today after down 90% over a quarter is not in an uptrend.
- **Narrative substitution** — a compelling story is not evidence. AI-agent
  branding tells you nothing about a token's supply schedule.
- **Survivorship in sources** — projects publish their good metrics. Cross-check
  self-reported figures against explorers and DefiLlama.
- **False precision on thin data** — quoting a price to four decimals when sources
  disagree by 30% implies a certainty that doesn't exist.
- **Fundamentals-to-token leap** — protocol growth does not imply token
  appreciation where value accrual is weak or emissions outpace demand.

## Portfolio-level pass

After the per-token work, step back:

- Which clusters carry the most exposure, and do the reads within them agree?
- What unlocks land across the whole list in the next 30 and 90 days, ordered by
  date and size?
- What changed materially since the last run?
- Where has data quality degraded — a source that used to agree and now doesn't
  may signal a supply change or a listing rather than a data bug.
- Which names have gone quiet? Absence of news on a small cap is itself a finding.
